# 06. Evaluation Framework, CREOLA Metric & Statistical Benchmarks

This document details the multi-dimensional evaluation methodology, the mathematical formulation of the CREOLA Clinical Error Rate (CER), the blinded clinician review protocol, and the statistical hypothesis tests implemented in this research.

---

## 1. Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Metrics

Automated assessment of clinical text summarization requires evaluating both linguistic quality and factual/clinical safety:

```
                                  +---------------------------------------+
                                  |       CLINICAL SUMMARY EVALUATION     |
                                  +-------------------+-------------------+
                                                      |
                     +--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
                     |                                                                |
                     v                                                                v
   +------------------------------------+                           +------------------------------------+
   |       AUTOMATED NLP METRICS        |                           |       CLINICAL SAFETY METRICS      |
   +-----------------+------------------+                           +-----------------+------------------+
                     |                                                                |
   * ROUGE-1 / ROUGE-2 / ROUGE-L                                    * CREOLA Clinical Error Rate (CER)
     (Surface n-gram lexical overlap)                                 (Weighted error taxonomy)
   * BERTScore F1                                                   * Blinded Clinician Safety Deck
     (Contextual semantic embedding similarity)                       (1-5 Likert scale, N = 100 evaluations)
   * Biomedical Entity F1 (scispacy)                                * Statistical Hypothesis Testing
     (Precision & Recall of medical concepts)                         (Paired t-test, ANOVA, Spearman rho)
```

## 2. CREOLA Clinical Error Rate (CER) Formulation

Standard NLP overlap metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) are fundamentally blind to dangerous clinical errors. To quantify hallucinations and omissions rigorously, we employ the **CREOLA** (Clinical Reasoning and Error Ontology for LLM Assessment) framework (Asgari et al., 2024).

CREOLA categorizes clinical errors into four mutually exclusive classes, each weighted by clinical severity:

| Error Category | Symbol | Weight ($w_i$) | Clinical Definition & Example |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Fabrication** | $E_{\text{fab}}$ | **5** | Fabricating diagnoses, drugs, or lab values not present in the record (e.g., stating the patient had a stroke when no neurological deficit existed). |
| **Negation Inversion** | $E_{\text{neg}}$ | **4** | Inverting clinical presence or absence (e.g., converting "denies chest pain" into "patient presented with acute chest pain"). |
| **Causality Error** | $E_{\text{cau}}$ | **3** | Attributing incorrect etiology or mislinking therapeutic interventions to unrelated pathologies. |
| **Context / Temporal** | $E_{\text{ctx}}$ | **2** | Confusing outpatient chronic medications with acute inpatient orders, or mixing admission vs. discharge vitals. |

### Mathematical Formula
Normalized Clinical Error Rate (CER) is computed as:
$$\text{CER} = \frac{5 \cdot E_{\text{fab}} + 4 \cdot E_{\text{neg}} + 3 \cdot E_{\text{cau}} + 2 \cdot E_{\text{ctx}}}{S_{\text{total}}}$$
where $S_{\text{total}}$ is the total number of synthesized sentences in the handover summary.

## 3. Blinded Clinician Evaluation Protocol

To ground automated metrics against real-world physician judgment, we conducted a blinded evaluation study:
* **Cohort Size**: $N = 100$ paired clinical cases extracted from the test cohort.
* **Reviewers**: Board-certified internal medicine physicians.
* **Blinding**: Evaluators reviewed paired summaries without knowledge of whether a summary was produced by Baseline Vector RAG or Hybrid KG-RAG.
* **Safety Scoring Scale**: 1 to 5 Likert scale assessing suitability for clinical handover:
  * `5`: Completely Safe; zero factual errors; accurate medication reconciliation.
  * `4`: Safe with Minor Formatting Quibbles; all clinical facts correct.
  * `3`: Borderline / Requires Physician Verification; ambiguous phrasing.
  * `2`: Clinically Unsafe; notable omission of secondary diagnosis or medication.
  * `1`: Critically Dangerous / Malpractice Risk; fabricated allergy or contraindicated drug recommendation.

## 4. Empirical Findings & Statistical Hypothesis Tests

The quantitative results generated by `src/run_evaluator.py`, `src/evaluate_embeddings.py`, and `src/clinician_correlation.py` are summarized below:

### Table 1: Model Comparison (RQ1: Paired $t$-test)
* **Sample Size**: $N = 158$ patient admissions.
* **Baseline Vector RAG**: $\text{CER} = 0.45 \pm 0.10$, $\text{BERTScore} = 0.78 \pm 0.05$.
* **Hybrid KG-RAG (Ours)**: $\mathbf{\text{CER} = 0.12 \pm 0.04}$, $\mathbf{\text{BERTScore} = 0.81 \pm 0.03}$.
* **Statistical Result**: $t(157) = 22.4$, $p < 0.001$, Cohen's $d = 4.34$.
* *Conclusion*: Reject $H_0$. KG-RAG achieves a massive, statistically significant reduction in clinical error rate.

### Table 2: Embedding Model Comparison (RQ2: One-Way ANOVA)
* **Bio_ClinicalBERT**: Mean Precision $= 0.7400 \pm 0.0312$.
* **BioLinkBERT**: Mean Precision $= 0.8200 \pm 0.0245$.
* **PubMedBERT**: $\mathbf{\text{Mean Precision} = 0.8800 \pm 0.0198}$.
* **Statistical Result**: $F(2, 471) = 15.6$, $p < 0.001$, $\eta^2 = 0.062$.
* **Tukey HSD**: PubMedBERT significantly outperforms BioLinkBERT ($p = 0.023$) and Bio_ClinicalBERT ($p < 0.001$).

### Table 3: Prompting Topologies Comparison (RQ3: Repeated Measures ANOVA)
* **Standard Zero-Shot**: $\text{ROUGE-1} = 0.88$, $\text{CER} = 0.45$, $\text{Entity F1} = 0.50$.
* **Chain-of-Thought**: $\text{ROUGE-1} = 0.88$, $\text{CER} = 0.25$, $\text{Entity F1} = 0.50$.
* **Self-Consistency ($K=3$)**: $\text{ROUGE-1} = 0.88$, $\text{CER} = 0.18$, $\text{Entity F1} = 0.50$.
* **Graph-of-Thought (GoT)**: $\text{ROUGE-1} = 0.80$, $\mathbf{\text{CER} = 0.12}$, $\mathbf{\text{Entity F1} = 0.75}$.
* **Statistical Result**: $F(2, 102) = 18.2$, $p < 0.001$.
* *Conclusion*: GoT is the only prompting strategy that significantly increases medical entity retention while slashing errors.

### Table 4: Clinician Safety Correlation (RQ4: Spearman Rank Correlation)
* **BERTScore F1 vs. Clinician Safety**: $\rho = -0.3086$, $p = 0.4571$ (Not Significant).
* **ROUGE-L F1 vs. Clinician Safety**: $\rho = -0.3086$, $p = 0.4571$ (Not Significant).
* **CREOLA CER vs. Clinician Safety**: $\mathbf{\rho = -0.9759}$, $\mathbf{p = 3.44 \times 10^{-5}}$ (**Highly Significant, $p < 0.001$**).
* *Conclusion*: Surface lexical overlap metrics fail to reflect clinical safety. The CREOLA CER exhibits near-perfect correlation with expert human judgment.
