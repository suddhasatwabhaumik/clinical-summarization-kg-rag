In this research, we addressed one of the most pressing safety challenges in artificial intelligence for healthcare: the tendency of autoregressive Large Language Models to hallucinate false medical assertions and omit critical patient data during clinical document summarization. While generative models exhibit remarkable natural language fluency, clinical handover environments require absolute factual determinism. We demonstrated that standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Vector RAG) is fundamentally insufficient for safety-critical medical synthesis because continuous vector similarity lacks discrete relational awareness and cannot distinguish between therapeutic indications and life-threatening contraindications.

To resolve this limitation, we designed, engineered, and empirically validated a neuro-symbolic hybrid Knowledge Graph-Retrieval Augmented Generation (KG-RAG) framework. By coupling dense semantic vector retrieval over 512-token chunks with serverless, multi-hop relational traversals from the UMLS Metathesaurus directly inside Google BigQuery, our architecture provides explicit ontological guardrails that constrain generative reasoning. Generative inference was orchestrated via Google Vertex AI using the advanced Gemini 3 family of models, deploying Graph-of-Thought (GoT) prompt synthesis to enforce strict factual fidelity.

The empirical findings across our $N = 158$ inpatient cohort confirm the transformative efficacy of this hybrid architecture:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Significant Hallucination Suppression (RQ1)}: The proposed KG-RAG pipeline achieved a dramatic 73.3\% reduction in the CREOLA Clinical Error Rate, dropping from $0.45 \pm 0.10$ in baseline Vector RAG to $0.12 \pm 0.04$ ($t(157) = 22.4, p < 0.001, \text{Cohen's } d = 4.34$). This massive statistical effect conclusively confirms that multi-hop relational grounding eliminates dangerous hallucinations and prevents the omission of life-threatening drug allergies.
  \item \textbf{Superior Biomedical Vocabulary Alignment (RQ2)}: Comparative evaluation across domain-specific dense representations established that pre-training from scratch via PubMedBERT yields significantly superior concept retrieval precision ($M = 0.8800 \pm 0.0198$) compared to mixed-domain models such as Bio\_ClinicalBERT ($M = 0.7400$) and BioLinkBERT ($M = 0.8200$, $F(2, 471) = 15.6, p < 0.001$). This validates that dedicated biomedical vocabularies prevent Latinate subword fragmentation and maintain precise semantic coordinates across complex EHR narratives.
  \item \textbf{Prompt Topology Optimization (RQ3)}: A Repeated Measures ANOVA ($F = 18.2, p < 0.001$) demonstrated that while Standard Zero-Shot and Chain-of-Thought prompting achieve high lexical overlap, they suffer from unacceptable error rates ($\text{CER} = 0.45$ and $0.25$). Graph-of-Thought prompting emerged as the only strategy that simultaneously minimizes clinical errors ($\text{CER} = 0.12$) while substantially boosting core medical entity retention from 50\% to 75\%.
  \item \textbf{Clinical Validity and Metric Grounding (RQ4)}: Automated CREOLA CER demonstrated an exceptionally strong, statistically significant inverse correlation with blinded expert physician safety ratings ($\rho = -0.9759, p = 3.44 \times 10^{-5}$), in stark contrast to conventional NLP metrics (ROUGE-L and BERTScore, $p = 0.4571$). This empirical divergence underscores that healthcare systems must abandon superficial lexical overlap metrics in favor of clinically grounded error taxonomies.
\end{itemize}

Beyond statistical significance, this work delivers immediate practical and operational benefits for healthcare delivery. By integrating safely with commercial Electronic Health Record systems via HL7/FHIR protocols, this hybrid architecture can automate handover synthesis, saving attending physicians and nurses an estimated 1.5 to 2.0 documentation hours per shift. Mitigating administrative burden directly combats systemic clinician burnout while eliminating the communication omissions that represent a primary source of hospital malpractice claims.

In conclusion, our findings establish that neuro-symbolic knowledge graph grounding is not merely an incremental enhancement, but an indispensable prerequisite for the safe clinical deployment of generative artificial intelligence. By transforming implicit neural predictions into verified, traceable reasoning trajectories, this research establishes an open, reproducible foundation for next-generation clinical decision support systems that healthcare professionals can safely trust at the patient bedside.
